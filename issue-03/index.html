The Take

August payrolls came in hot at +162K, beating consensus by over 100K. July was revised up 44K, from −23K to +21K, so the contraction we flagged in Issue 02 was corrected to growth. But JOLTS still showed low quits (1.9%) and a 278K drop in hires even as openings rose. Employers are posting roles, not filling them.

Enablement held through all of it. Volume flat, comp firm at $157K median. Issue 01 asked what enablement pays. Issue 02 asked what it is. This issue asks what actually drives the number.

With 512 roles disclosing comp and full JD text, we can now test every skill, tool, and work arrangement against pay. AI fluency carries the largest all-levels premium (+$15K), followed by revenue outcomes (+$9K) and coaching (+$8K). But at the Senior level, coaching flips to the top at +$22K. And naming specific tools (a CMS, CRM, LMS) correlates with lower comp, not higher.

Then there's presence. Remote enablement roles pay $5–19K less than non-remote, at every seniority level. The biggest differentiators are the oldest ones: can you coach, and will you show up?

The comp advantage isn't where everyone's looking.

The premium map

Median comp premium by skill or tool mention vs. roles without it · n=512 with comp

Three skills pay more: AI (+$15K), revenue outcomes (+$9K), and coaching (+$8K). Everything else pays less. Naming specific tools, a CMS, CRM, or an LMS, correlates with $9–29K lower comp. The same goes for onboarding-focused roles (−$16K) and methodology-heavy JDs that reference MEDDIC or Challenger (−$14K).

The more a JD reads like a tool-operator spec, the lower it pays. The more it reads like a role shaping how sellers sell, the higher.

Premium = median comp for roles mentioning a skill minus median comp for roles that don't. Computed across 512 roles with comp in the frozen August 2026 dataset. Cross-sectional correlations, not controlled experiments.

The human skill leads

Comp premium by skill, Senior-level roles only (n=203) · controlled for seniority

When we control for seniority and look at Senior-level roles only, coaching carries the largest premium at +$22K. AI is second at +$19K. Revenue outcomes third at +$13K. Coaching appears in just 35% of Senior JDs, while AI is in 72%, but the scarcer skill pays more.

What does coaching look like in these roles? It’s not classroom training. It’s being in live deals, shaping seller behavior in the moment, running call reviews, building muscle memory. The JDs that mention coaching describe someone in the room changing how people sell, not someone building a deck about it.

Premiums computed within the Senior seniority band (titles containing senior, lead, principal, sr.). This controls for the obvious fact that Directors earn more than Specialists. Coaching = JD mentions “coach” or “coaching.”

Showing up pays

Median comp by seniority, remote vs. not remote · n=512

At every level of the ladder, non-remote roles pay more than remote. The gap is $5–20K depending on seniority. A remote Specialist earns $106K; the same role in-person or hybrid pays $125K. At Director+, it’s $196K vs. $216K.

This isn’t a seniority proxy, it’s the same jobs paying differently based on where you sit. Remote work is traded for flexibility. That flexibility has a price.

Work mode classified from JD text (mentions of “hybrid,” “remote,” “on-site,” “days in office”), not just the location field. “Not remote” includes hybrid, in-person, and city-listed roles where the JD doesn’t specify remote. 41% of roles are hybrid, 29% remote, 4% explicitly in-person.

The OTE reality check

On-target earnings across GTM roles · enablement highlighted · RepVue + Enablement Index

Enablement at ~$157K sits below every quota-carrying role. An Account Executive targets $200K. An Enterprise AE targets $280K. But only about 42% of AEs hit quota. Risk-adjusted, enablement comp looks a lot like the typical rep’s realized pay. Certain money on one side, a lottery ticket on the other.

The market rewards people who change behavior, not people who operate platforms. AI matters, but the biggest premium in the dataset belongs to coaching, the one thing you still have to do in person.

The Bottom Line

Across all levels, AI leads the premium map at +$15K, with revenue outcomes (+$9K) and coaching (+$8K) following. But at the Senior level, coaching flips to the top at +$22K, ahead of AI (+$19K) and revenue outcomes (+$13K). AI fluency matters. But the skill that pays the most at the Senior level is the one you can't automate.

Meanwhile, the tool-operator layer, the roles that name a CMS, CRM, or an LMS in the JD, pays $9–29K less than roles that don't. Methodology-heavy JDs (MEDDIC, Challenger) pay $14K less. Onboarding-focused roles pay $16K less. The more tactical and platform-specific the job, the lower it pays.

And then there's showing up. Remote roles pay $5–20K less than non-remote at every seniority level. That gap isn't explained by industry, segment, or title. Flexibility has a price.

Everyone's talking about what AI will replace. Nobody's talking about what it already can't do. The data is.

How we counted

We collected 784 roles with "enablement" in the title from six public ATS platforms (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, SmartRecruiters, Workday, Built In) through August 2026. After deduplication and filtering, 679 made the clean set. 512 disclose both compensation and full JD text, forming the analytical sample. Comp premiums are computed as: median comp for roles whose JD mentions a skill, minus median comp for roles whose JD does not. To control for seniority, we split roles into four bands by title (Specialist/IC, Manager, Senior, Director+) and recompute within each. Work mode (remote, hybrid, in-person) is classified from JD text, not just the location field. These are cross-sectional correlations, not controlled experiments: a role that mentions coaching may differ in other ways too. AE and sales role OTE from RepVue. Market context from BLS Employment Situation, JOLTS, Indeed Hiring Lab, and the RepVue Cloud Sales Index.
