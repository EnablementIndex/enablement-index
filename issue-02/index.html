What is enablement? It depends who you ask.

We clustered 481 job descriptions by language. At least five different genres of work share the same title. The boundaries between them are blurry, and someone else might draw the lines differently. But the data can show what they have in common and where they diverge.

The Take

July payrolls came in at −23K, the first negative print in months. The prior two months were revised down by a combined 103K. Unemployment ticked down to 4.1%, but only because people left the labor force. Indeed's posting index sits at 102, barely above its pre-pandemic baseline. The broad economy isn't crashing, but it isn't hiring.

Issue 01 looked at what enablement pays. This issue looks at what enablement is. We ran TF-IDF across 481 full job descriptions and clustered them by language similarity. Five genres came out. Someone else could draw the lines differently, and the boundaries are genuinely blurry, but the comp differences between genres are not.

Here are the five, where we drew the lines, and what each one looks like.

Technical Enablement $170K

These roles arm technical sellers and partners with product depth, demo environments, and solution frameworks. The entry barrier is specialist knowledge, not sales experience.

  • Senior Technical Enablement Manager
  • AI Enablement Lead
  • Director of Enablement, Solutions Engineering
n=61
Enterprise Ops / Governance $163K

Enablement built around risk, compliance, and operational governance in regulated industries. These roles sit inside financial services, insurance, and large enterprise operations where controls matter as much as revenue.

  • AI Implementation Enablement, Client Segment Risk VP
  • Business Process Enablement Manager
  • Associate Director, Governance & Data Management
n=119
Life Sciences / Clinical $158K

Enablement in healthcare, medtech, and pharma, where clinical vocabulary and regulatory knowledge shape the job. These roles train field teams on diagnostics, devices, and commercial launch.

  • Director, Sales Training & Enablement – Abbott Vascular
  • Field Sales Trainer
  • Director, Site Enablement – Oncology
n=63
Sales & GTM $154K

The closest thing to "classic" enablement: onboarding new reps, coaching on methodology, building pipeline playbooks, and shortening ramp time. These roles live inside or directly adjacent to the sales org.

  • Head of Sales Enablement
  • GTM Enablement Lead
  • Enterprise Revenue Enablement Manager
n=114
Learning & Customer $152K

The outward-facing side of enablement: designing learning programs, enabling customers and partners, and driving adoption post-sale. These roles often report into customer success or product rather than sales.

  • Director, Customer Enablement
  • Customer Support Learning & Enablement Specialist
  • Revenue Enablement Program Manager
n=124

If there's one thing every genre shares, it's coordination. 85% of all enablement JDs mention cross-functional or stakeholder work. Nothing else clears 65%. Training is at 54%, content 47%, coaching 44%. The title changes, the org chart changes, but nearly every version of the role expects the person to work across teams.

Cross-functional coordination, not training, is the defining act of enablement. It appears in 85% of JDs. Everything else, training, content, coaching, tools, clusters between 35% and 54%. No single deliverable defines the function; coordination across GTM teams does.

Genres are identified by running TF-IDF across all 481 JDs and clustering by language similarity (k-means, k=5). They overlap (silhouette score ≈ 0.06), which means enablement is a spectrum, not five clean partitions. Genre names come from each cluster's distinguishing terms. Comp coverage ranges from n=35 (Technical) to n=80 (Enterprise Ops, Sales & GTM); medians are of disclosed postings only.

The map

Each dot is a JD, positioned by language similarity · t-SNE on TF-IDF, k=5, n=481

This is one way to draw the map. The clusters bleed into each other. Life Sciences and Technical Enablement separate most clearly, while Sales & GTM, Learning & Customer, and Enterprise Ops / Governance share a broad middle. A different clustering method, or a different number of clusters, would draw different boundaries through the same cloud. The genres are a lens, not a taxonomy. Positions on the scatter carry no unit meaning, only relative distance matters.

Issue 01 found that agentic AI carried a $23K comp premium. Whether that premium holds as the term becomes more common is something to track in future issues.

The Bottom Line

Enablement means different things at different companies. When you cluster 481 JDs by the language in them, at least five genres emerge, but the lines between them are soft, and a different algorithm or a different k would draw them somewhere else. What's consistent: the genre that requires deep specialization (Technical, $170K) pays more than the broadest, most outward-facing genre (Learning & Customer, $152K). The spread is $18K. Enablement pays in a tight band. What varies isn't the money, it's the work.

Issue 01 asked what enablement pays. This issue asks what it is. The honest answer is that it depends on the company, the industry, the buyer, the org chart, and which problems the team was hired to fix. The data can't tell you which genre is the right one, but it can tell you they all pay within $18K of each other and they're all called the same thing.

How we counted

We collected 601 roles with "enablement" in the title from six public ATS platforms (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, SmartRecruiters, Workday, Built In) through July 2026. After filtering out non-aligned titles and keeping only roles with full job description text, 481 JDs made the cut. We used AI-assisted analysis to vectorize the text of every JD (TF-IDF, stripping boilerplate like EEO and benefits sections first), reduce the dimensions, and cluster them with k-means. The algorithm tried several values of k. Five gave the most interpretable genres. The clusters overlap heavily (silhouette ≈ 0.06), which is the point: these are genres on a spectrum, not clean categories.
