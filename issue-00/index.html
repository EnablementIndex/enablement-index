The Take

In early 2025 we hand-collected revenue-enablement job postings off LinkedIn and company career pages. After cleaning, we had just under a hundred roles where both the salary and the full job description were captured. Not an optimal sample size, but big enough to see the shape.

What we saw: same title, different pay. An "Enablement Manager" could earn $100K or $200K. The variable wasn't seniority, industry, or reporting line. It was the language. Roles built around revenue attribution averaged $150K. Roles built around tool ownership averaged $112K. Same job function with different skills in the JD brought different numbers on the offer.

That insight became the editorial spine of the Enablement Index: the market doesn't pay for activities, it pays for outcomes. Issue 00 publishes the original 2024–25 baseline in the new format. Issue 01 reads the year that followed.

Revenue Attribution lifts every skill it touches. Tool Ownership alone is a $112K job; Tool Ownership + Revenue Attribution is a $161K job — the highest cell in the matrix. LMS, Data Literacy, Cross-Functional Collaboration all jump $30–50K when paired with revenue language. The one pairing that doesn't appear at all is Revenue Attribution × Proof of Value — those two terms never showed up in the same JD. The hiring managers who write for revenue attribution don't write for proof of value like ROI. Lower-frequency pairings carry small samples, read directionally not exactly.

The Signal

There are more open CRO positions than VPs of Enablement.

That was Kunal Pandya's line when we first presented this data in 2025 on Sales Velocity Labs and the SEC Summit, and the data agreed with him. Enablement reads to most leadership teams as important but not essential.

It's why the pay ladder tops out where it does, why VP titles are rare on public boards, and why "revenue attribution" earns a premium. It's this language that breaks the cost-center framing enablement gets trapped in. Issue 01 will look at this data in 2026.

How we counted

Issue 00 reads the original 2024–25 dataset as it was. Hundreds of revenue-enablement postings were tracked off LinkedIn and company career pages through early 2025; fewer than a hundred had both salary data and full job descriptions captured. That data set is the analytical core for every chart in this issue. Clusters were assigned via KMeans on TF-IDF features of the JD text; skill tags were extracted from JD language and joined to posted compensation (base or midpoint of disclosed range; OTE where listed). Comp values are averages, not median. This is a deliberate choice to stay consistent with how this data was originally reported; future issues of the Enablement Index will standardize on medians.

The sample is small. It is also the reason the Enablement Index exists. A hand-built dataset can't scale to monthly tracking, and the gaps in this baseline — manager-heavy tagging, limited VP coverage, no macro context — are exactly what the Index was built to fix. Future issues will bring in data closer to real time on a much larger and more robust dataset.

This data was originally analyzed in Q1 2025 and co-presented with Kunal Pandya (Sales Velocity Labs). Compensation figures reflect posted base or midpoint of disclosed range; OTE noted where applicable. Skill tags extracted from job description text. n < 100 with full JD & salary.
