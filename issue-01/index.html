The market pays for outcomes, not activities.

The Take

The broad U.S. labor market is frozen. June payrolls came in at +57K, the weakest growth of 2026, with the two prior months revised down a combined 74K. Indeed's job posting index is pinned at 101, essentially where it sat before the pandemic. Real wages are losing to inflation — average hourly earnings up 3.5% against CPI at 4.2%, a −0.7% real loss.

The sales floor has it worse. RepVue's Q1 2026 index shows only 44% of SaaS reps hitting quota. Inbound lead flow sentiment sits at 2.70 out of 5, the lowest read since mid-2025. Quota attainment has been stuck in the 40s for nine consecutive quarters — mostly flat, only recently trending up. The majority of sales reps are underperforming, and have been for over two years.

Enablement is running the other way. Median listed comp is up 14% YoY. Manager pay is up 17%. Enablement job volume is growing while quota attainment keeps dropping. Most of what happens in enablement — content libraries, learning paths, tool rollouts, methodology certifications — isn't what the market is paying for. The pay premium sits on roles that name revenue impact: win rate, ramp, pipeline. Most enablement teams are still built around the wrong outputs.

The broad economy is at "low-hire, low-fire." The sales floor is chronically missing. Enablement pay is up double digits and hiring volume is growing. Read together, the pattern suggests demand: sales orgs can't hit numbers, and the people who help them close are the ones the market still wants to pay for.

Discounts are grouped: Content Mgmt Platforms bundles Highspot, Seismic, Showpad, Mindtickle, Guru (Seismic and Highspot have announced merger intent). Named Methodology bundles MEDDIC, Challenger, Force Management, Sandler, SPIN. The pattern is clear at the extremes: outcome language (win rate, ramp time) carries the highest premiums; platform and tool language carries the deepest discounts. Senior roles disproportionately name specific outcomes (Win Rate 38% Director+, Ramp Time 44% Director+); execution roles disproportionately name the platforms and frameworks used to do the work. The spread from Win Rate (+$39K) to Content Mgmt Platforms (−$21K) is $60K on the same job function.

Call intelligence is the only category still growing inside enablement. Gong went from 11.0% of JDs to 12.0%. Everything else the enablement JD used to name is fading. Highspot and Seismic just announced merger intent, so we're counting them as one platform — combined they dropped from 16.5% to 11.3%, category share shrinking while the vendors consolidate (the tell of a maturing market). The sequencer layer isn't losing to another tool inside enablement, it's leaving enablement entirely: Outreach 6.6% → 2.9%, SalesLoft 6.6% → 0.4%. That work likely sits in demand gen, marketing ops, or the emerging GTM-engineering roles now. The JDs simply stop appearing in enablement datasets, so this remains a hypothesis until we can cross-check other functions. One more thing worth flagging: the PE M&A activity around SalesLoft (acquisition, Drift merger, rebrand) seems to have driven demand away from the tool rather than toward it, a pattern worth watching as more of the category consolidates.

82% of open roles sit at the execution tiers (Specialist and Manager). But the pay premium runs the other way: outcome and strategy language lifts comp by up to $39K, while tool and process language discounts it by as much as $21K. Most of the headcount goes to execution, but most of the money goes to strategy. The leadership layer is nearly invisible on public boards, likely filled by promotion rather than posting.

The Bottom Line

The market hires for execution and pays for strategy. 84% of open enablement roles sit at the Specialist and Manager tiers, but the comp premium goes to roles that name revenue outcomes, not the ones that name tools and certifications. The $60K spread between Win Rate and Content Management Platforms is the clearest read on where the function is headed.

If you run an enablement team, the implication is structural: the JDs your company posts tell the market whether you're building around activities or outcomes, and the comp you're able to offer follows accordingly. If you're building an enablement career, the signal is the same. The skills that pay more are the ones closest to revenue. How we counted

Sample: 408 roles with "enablement" in the title, gathered from six public ATS platforms (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, SmartRecruiters, Workday, Built In) as of early July 2026. The initial sample is unfiltered by function, so the raw set includes IT enablement, platform enablement, security enablement, and other non-sales roles. After limiting to roles aligned with a sales, revenue, or GTM function, the clean analytical set is 284 roles, of which 193 disclose compensation. Cross-referenced against the BLS Employment Situation, JOLTS, Indeed Hiring Lab, and the RepVue Cloud Sales Index for sales-org context. Year-over-year baseline is a 91-role 2024–25 dataset. Segments are assigned by title normalization (Specialist / Manager / Director+). Compensation reflects the salary listed on the posting; postings vary in whether that figure is base only, base plus variable, or total target, and the specifics often aren't clear until deeper in the process. Skill deltas compare median pay for roles that name a term vs. those that don't. This is a tech-sector index; non-tech employers post on ATS platforms not yet in the dataset. Issue 01 is the baseline; month-over-month tracking begins as the dataset stabilizes.
